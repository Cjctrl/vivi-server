# Bokeh

For other uses, see [Bokeh (disambiguation)](Bokeh_\(disambiguation\) "Bokeh \(disambiguation\)").

Not to be confused with [Depth of field](Depth_of_field "Depth of field").

In [photography](Photography "Photography"), **bokeh** (/ˈboʊkə/ _BOH -kə_ or /ˈboʊkeɪ/ _BOH -kay_; Japanese: [boke]) is the aesthetic quality of the blur produced in [out-of-focus](Defocus_aberration "Defocus aberration") parts of an image, whether foreground or background or both. It is created by using a wide aperture lens. 

Some photographers incorrectly restrict use of the term bokeh to the appearance of bright spots in the out-of-focus area caused by [circles of confusion](Circle_of_confusion "Circle of confusion"). Bokeh has also been defined as "the way the [lens](Lens "Lens") renders out-of-focus points of light". Differences in [lens aberrations](Optical_aberration "Optical aberration") and [aperture](Aperture "Aperture") shape cause very different bokeh effects. Some lens designs blur the image in a way that is pleasing to the eye, while others produce distracting or unpleasant blurring ("good" and "bad" bokeh, respectively). Photographers may deliberately use a [shallow focus](Shallow_focus "Shallow focus") technique to create images with prominent out-of-focus regions, accentuating their lens's bokeh. 

Bokeh is often most visible around small background [highlights](Point_source#Light "Point source"), such as [specular reflections](Specular_reflection "Specular reflection") and [light sources](List_of_light_sources "List of light sources"), which is why it is often associated with such areas. However, bokeh is not limited to highlights; blur occurs in all regions of an image which are outside the [depth of field](Depth_of_field "Depth of field"). 

The opposite of bokeh—an image in which multiple distances are visible and all are in focus—is [deep focus](Deep_focus "Deep focus").
