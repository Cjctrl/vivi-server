### 🤖 Claude · 2025-06-01 15:27

I'll answer your questions about female reproductive health:

**1. Which organ transports eggs from the ovaries to the uterus?**
The fallopian tubes transport eggs from the ovaries to the uterus. These tubes have tiny hair-like structures called cilia that help move the egg along the pathway.

**2. Why is the vagina sometimes called the birth canal?**
The vagina is called the birth canal because it's the passageway through which a baby travels during vaginal delivery. During childbirth, the vagina stretches significantly to allow the baby to pass from the uterus to the outside world.

**3. What change in the body causes menstruation?**
Menstruation occurs when hormone levels (particularly estrogen and progesterone) drop sharply. This happens when pregnancy doesn't occur, causing the thickened lining of the uterus (endometrium) that was prepared for a potential pregnancy to shed through the vagina.

**4. What is the difference between ovarian cysts and ovarian cancer?**
Ovarian cysts are usually benign (non-cancerous) fluid-filled sacs that form on or in the ovaries. Most are harmless and often disappear on their own. Ovarian cancer involves malignant (cancerous) cells that grow uncontrollably and can spread to other parts of the body. Cysts are very common, while ovarian cancer is much rarer but more serious.

**5. Why should females wipe from front to back after urinating?**
Wiping from front to back prevents bacteria from the anal area from being transferred to the urethra and vagina. This helps prevent urinary tract infections (UTIs) and vaginal infections, since bacteria from the intestinal tract can cause problems in the urinary and reproductive systems.

**6. What kind of doctor specializes in female reproductive health?**
An obstetrician-gynecologist (OB-GYN) specializes in female reproductive health. Obstetricians focus on pregnancy and childbirth, while gynecologists focus on the female reproductive system, including routine care, infections, and reproductive health issues. Many doctors are trained in both areas (OB-GYN).
