# Graph Theory

Graph theory is the study of graphs, which are mathematical structures used to model pairwise relations between objects. A graph in this context is made up of vertices (also called nodes or points) which are connected by edges (also called links or lines).

## Basic Concepts
- **Vertices (Nodes)**: The fundamental units of graphs
- **Edges (Links)**: Connections between vertices
- **Directed vs Undirected**: Whether edges have a direction
- **Weighted vs Unweighted**: Whether edges have associated weights
- **Paths and Cycles**: Sequences of vertices connected by edges

## Applications in Computer Science
- **Network analysis**: Social networks, computer networks, biological networks
- **Routing algorithms**: Finding shortest paths in networks
- **Data structures**: Trees, heaps, and other graph-based structures
- **Machine Learning**: Graph neural networks, spectral clustering, community detection

## Graph Neural Networks
Graph neural networks (GNNs) are a class of deep learning methods designed to perform inference on data described by graphs. They can handle various types of graphs and are particularly useful for:
- Social network analysis
- Molecular chemistry and drug discovery
- Recommendation systems
- Knowledge graph reasoning

Graph theory provides the mathematical foundation for many algorithms and data structures used in computer science, including applications in artificial intelligence and machine learning.
