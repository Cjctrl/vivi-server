# Stimulated Emission

Stimulated emission is the process by which an incoming photon of a specific frequency can interact with an excited atomic electron (or other excited molecular state), causing it to drop to a lower energy level. The liberated energy transfers to the electromagnetic field, creating a new photon with a phase, frequency, polarization, and direction of travel that are all identical to the photons of the incident wave.

## Fundamental Concept
This process is fundamentally different from spontaneous emission, which occurs randomly without external influence. In stimulated emission:
- **Incident Photon**: Triggers the emission
- **Identical Photon**: The emitted photon is identical to the incident photon in phase, frequency, polarization, and direction
- **Coherence**: Results in coherent light amplification

## Laser Principle
Stimulated emission is the fundamental physical process that allows lasers (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation) to operate. For laser action to occur:
1. Population inversion must be achieved (more atoms in excited state than ground state)
2. Stimulated emission exceeds absorption
3. Optical feedback provides coherence (typically via mirrors in an optical cavity)

## Applications
- Laser technology (cutting, welding, medical procedures, communications)
- Optical amplifiers in fiber optics
- Scientific research instruments
- Barcode scanners and laser pointers
- Optical storage (CD, DVD, Blu-ray)

While stimulated emission is primarily a physics concept, its technological applications in lasers and optical systems have numerous intersections with computer science, including optical data storage, fiber-optic communications, laser printing, and various sensing technologies used in computer systems and robotics.
