# Theodora (wife of Theophilos)

Theodora (Greek: Te?d??a) was a Byzantine Empress as the wife of Emperor Theophilos, and the regent for her son Michael III from 842 to 855. She is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church for her role in the restoration of the icons, which ended the second period of Byzantine iconoclasm.

## Life

### Early Life
Theodora was born around 815 into a noble Armenian family in Paphlagonia, on the southern coast of the Black Sea. Her father was Marinos, a commander of a naval regiment, and her mother was Theoktiste. The family was prominent in the region and had connections to the imperial court.

### Marriage to Theophilos
In 830, Theodora was selected in a bride-show to become the wife of the young Emperor Theophilos, who was then 16 years old. The bride-show was a Byzantine imperial custom where eligible young women from noble families were presented to the emperor, who would choose his wife from among them. Theodora's selection was likely influenced by her beauty, intelligence, and noble background.

### Reign as Empress
As empress, Theodora was known for her piety and devotion to the Orthodox Christian faith, which put her at odds with her husband Theophilos, who was an ardent iconoclast. Despite their religious differences, the couple had several children together:
- Thekla (born c. 830)
- Anna (born c. 832)
- Anastasia (born c. 833)
- Maria (born c. 837)
- Michael III (born January 19, 840), who would become emperor

## Regency for Michael III

### Accession and Regency
When Theophilos died on January 20, 842, their son Michael III was only two years old. Theodora was proclaimed regent for her minor son, effectively ruling the Byzantine Empire on his behalf. She governed with the help of a council that included her brothers Bardas and Petronas, the eunuch Theoktistos, and the minister Manuel.

### Restoration of the Icons
The most significant act of Theodora's regency was the restoration of the veneration of icons in 847, which ended the second period of Byzantine iconoclasm that had begun under Leo V in 813. This event is known as the Triumph of Orthodoxy and is celebrated annually on the first Sunday of Great Lent in the Eastern Orthodox Church.

According to tradition, Theodora secretly kept icons in her chambers despite her husband's prohibition. After his death, she openly brought them back into use, first in the private chapels of the palace and then in public churches. The restoration was formalized by a synod held in Constantinople in March 847, which anathematized iconoclasm and reaffirmed the decisions of the Second Council of Nicaea (787).

### Governance and Foreign Policy
During her regency, Theodora's administration was generally successful:
- The empire's finances were stabilized through careful management
- The navy was strengthened and achieved victories against the Arabs
- Missionary work continued, particularly among the Slavs
- Diplomatic relations were maintained with Western powers, including the Carolingian Empire and the Papacy

However, there were also challenges:
- Ongoing conflicts with the Abbasid Caliphate on the eastern frontier
- Internal revolts, including one led by a relative named Theodotos in 843
- Court intrigues, particularly involving her brother Bardas, who would eventually overthrow her regency

## End of Regency and Later Life

### Overthrow by Bardas
In 855, Theodora's brother Bardas, with the support of Michael III (who was now about 15 years old), engineered a coup that ended Theodora's regency. Bardas accused her of plotting against the emperor and of adultery, though these charges were likely pretexts for his desire to seize power. Theodora was relegated to a monastery, possibly the Gastria Monastery near Constantinople.

### Monastic Life and Death
Theodora spent the remainder of her life in monastic seclusion. She died sometime after 867, though the exact date is uncertain. She was buried in the Gastria Monastery, which became a site of pilgrimage due to her reputation for piety and her role in restoring the icons.

## Legacy

### Sainthood
Theodora is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church. Her feast day is celebrated on February 11 in the Greek Orthodox Church and on February 12 in the Slavic Orthodox Churches. She is honored for her piety, her defense of Orthodox doctrine, and her role in ending iconoclasm.

### Historical Significance
Theodora's regency represents a critical period in Byzantine history:
- The restoration of orthodoxy helped stabilize the empire religiously after decades of conflict
- Her effective governance maintained the empire's strength during a challenging period
- She ensured the succession of her son, whose reign would see both cultural flourishing and eventual decline
- Her actions demonstrated the significant political influence that Byzantine empresses could wield

### Cultural Depictions
Theodora has been depicted in various forms of art and literature:
- Icons showing her holding a cross or an icon
- Historical chronicles such as those by Genesios and Theodorus the Reader
- Modern historical novels and academic works
- Church services and hymns commemorating the Triumph of Orthodoxy
