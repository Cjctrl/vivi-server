**Albert Einstein** (14 March 1879 – 18 April 1955) was a German-born [theoretical physicist](Theoretical_physicist "Theoretical physicist") best known for developing the [theory of relativity](Theory_of_relativity "Theory of relativity"). Einstein also made important contributions to [quantum theory](Old_quantum_theory "Old quantum theory"). His [mass–energy equivalence](Mass%E2%80%93energy_equivalence "Mass–energy equivalence") formula [_E_ = _mc_ 2](Mass%E2%80%93energy_equivalence#Mass–velocity_relationship "Mass–energy equivalence"), which arises from [special relativity](Special_relativity "Special relativity"), has been called "the world's most famous equation". He received the 1921 [Nobel Prize in Physics](Nobel_Prize_in_Physics "Nobel Prize in Physics") for "his services to theoretical physics, and especially for his discovery of the law of the [photoelectric effect](Photoelectric_effect "Photoelectric effect")". 

Born in the [German Empire](German_Empire "German Empire"), Einstein moved to [Switzerland](Switzerland "Switzerland") in 1895, forsaking his German citizenship the following year. In 1897, at the age of seventeen, he enrolled in the mathematics and physics teaching diploma program at the Swiss [federal polytechnic school](ETH_Zurich "ETH Zurich") in [Zurich](Zurich "Zurich"), graduating in 1900. He acquired Swiss citizenship a year later, which he kept for the rest of his life, and afterwards secured a permanent position at the [Swiss Patent Office](Swiss_Patent_Office "Swiss Patent Office") in [Bern](Bern "Bern"). In 1905, he submitted a successful PhD dissertation to the [University of Zurich](University_of_Zurich "University of Zurich"). In 1914, he moved to Berlin to join the [Prussian Academy of Sciences](Prussian_Academy_of_Sciences "Prussian Academy of Sciences") and the [Humboldt University of Berlin](Humboldt_University_of_Berlin "Humboldt University of Berlin"), becoming director of the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics](Kaiser_Wilhelm_Institute_for_Physics "Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics") in 1917; he also became a German citizen again. In 1933, while Einstein was visiting the United States, [Adolf Hitler came to power](Adolf_Hitler's_rise_to_power "Adolf Hitler's rise to power") in Germany. Horrified by the [Nazi](Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") persecution of his fellow [Jews](Jews "Jews"), he decided to remain in the US, and was granted American citizenship in 1940. On the eve of [World War II](World_War_II "World War II"), he endorsed [a letter](Einstein%E2%80%93Szilard_letter "Einstein–Szilard letter") to President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") alerting him to the potential [German nuclear weapons program](German_nuclear_weapons_program "German nuclear weapons program") and recommending that the US begin similar research, later carried out as the [Manhattan Project](Manhattan_Project "Manhattan Project"). 

In 1905, sometimes described as his _[annus mirabilis](Annus_mirabilis "Annus mirabilis")_ (miracle year), he published [four groundbreaking papers](Annus_mirabilis_papers "Annus mirabilis papers"). In them, he outlined a theory of the photoelectric effect, explained [Brownian motion](Brownian_motion "Brownian motion"), introduced his special theory of relativity, and demonstrated that if the special theory is correct, mass and energy are equivalent to each other. In 1915, he proposed a [general theory of relativity](General_theory_of_relativity "General theory of relativity") that extended his system of mechanics to incorporate [gravitation](Gravitation "Gravitation"). A paper that he published the following year laid out the implications of general relativity for the modeling of the structure and evolution of the [universe](Universe "Universe") as a whole. It introduced the [cosmological constant](Cosmological_constant "Cosmological constant") and is further regarded as the first step in the field of modern theoretical [cosmology](Cosmology "Cosmology"). In 1917, Einstein wrote a paper which introduced the concepts of [spontaneous emission](Spontaneous_emission "Spontaneous emission") and [stimulated emission](Stimulated_emission "Stimulated emission"), the latter of which is the core mechanism behind the [laser](Laser "Laser") and [maser](Maser "Maser"), and which contained a trove of information that would be beneficial to developments in physics later on, such as [quantum electrodynamics](Quantum_electrodynamics "Quantum electrodynamics") and [quantum optics](Quantum_optics "Quantum optics"). 

In the middle part of his career, Einstein made important contributions to [statistical mechanics](Statistical_mechanics "Statistical mechanics") and quantum theory. Especially notable was his work on the quantum physics of [radiation](Radiation "Radiation"), in which light consists of particles, subsequently called [photons](Photon "Photon"). With physicist [Satyendra Nath Bose](Satyendra_Nath_Bose "Satyendra Nath Bose"), he laid the groundwork for [Bose–Einstein statistics](Bose%E2%80%93Einstein_statistics "Bose–Einstein statistics"). For much of the last phase of his academic life, Einstein worked on two endeavors that ultimately proved unsuccessful. First, he advocated against quantum theory's introduction of fundamental randomness into science's picture of the world, objecting that "God does not play dice". Second, he attempted to devise a [unified field theory](Unified_field_theory "Unified field theory") by generalizing his geometric theory of gravitation to include [electromagnetism](Electromagnetism "Electromagnetism"). As a result, he became increasingly isolated from mainstream [modern physics](Modern_physics "Modern physics"). Many [things are named after him](List_of_things_named_after_Albert_Einstein "List of things named after Albert Einstein"), including the element [Einsteinium](Einsteinium "Einsteinium"). In 1999, he was named [_Time_](Time_\(magazine\) "Time \(magazine\)")'s [ Person of the Century](Time_100%3A_The_Most_Important_People_of_the_Century "Time 100: The Most Important People of the Century").
