## From today's featured article

Coin depicting Zenobia as empress

**[Zenobia](Zenobia "Zenobia")** (c. 240 – c. 274) was the queen of the [Palmyrene Empire](Palmyrene_Empire "Palmyrene Empire"). Her husband [Odaenathus](Odaenathus "Odaenathus") became king in 260 and elevated [Palmyra](Palmyra "Palmyra")'s power in the [Near East](Near_East "Near East") by defeating the [Sassanians](Sasanian_dynasty "Sasanian dynasty") and stabilizing the Roman East. After his assassination, she became the [regent](Regent "Regent") of her son [Vaballathus](Vaballathus "Vaballathus") and held de facto power throughout his reign. In 270, Zenobia launched an invasion which brought most of the Roman East under her sway, culminating with the annexation of Egypt. In reaction to Roman emperor [Aurelian](Aurelian "Aurelian")'s campaign in 272, Zenobia declared Palmyra's secession from Rome, naming her son emperor and assuming the title of empress. The Romans were victorious after heavy fighting; the queen was besieged in her capital and captured by Aurelian. He exiled her to Rome, where she spent the remainder of her life. Zenobia fostered a multicultural and intellectual environment in her court, which was open to scholars and philosophers. Her biography has inspired historians, artists and novelists. _(**[Full article...](Zenobia "Zenobia")**)_

## Today's featured picture

|  **[Lauren Bacall](Lauren_Bacall "Lauren Bacall")** (1924–2014) was an American actress. One of the last surviving major stars from the [Golden Age of Hollywood](Classical_Hollywood_cinema "Classical Hollywood cinema"), she was named the [20th-greatest female star](AFI's_100_Years...100_Stars "AFI's 100 Years...100 Stars") of classic Hollywood cinema by the [American Film Institute](American_Film_Institute "American Film Institute"), and received an [Academy Honorary Award](Academy_Honorary_Award "Academy Honorary Award") in 2009 in recognition of her contribution to the Golden Age of motion pictures. Bacall made her acting debut on [Broadway](Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre") in 1942 at the age of 17, and her final role was as a guest voice appearance in 2014. During her long career, she won seven major awards: one [Oscar](Academy_Awards "Academy Awards"), one [Critics' Choice Award](Critics'_Choice_Awards "Critics' Choice Awards"), two [Golden Globes](Golden_Globes "Golden Globes"), one [Screen Actors Guild Award](Actor_Awards "Actor Awards"), and two [Tony Awards](Tony_Awards "Tony Awards"). This photograph, taken by [Bernard Gotfryd](Bernard_Gotfryd "Bernard Gotfryd") circa 1979, shows Bacall with the [manuscript](Manuscript "Manuscript") of her first autobiography, _Lauren Bacall by Myself_ , at the offices of the publishing house [Alfred A.
