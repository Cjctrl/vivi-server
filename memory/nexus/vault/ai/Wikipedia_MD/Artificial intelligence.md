Glossary

  * [Glossary](Glossary_of_artificial_intelligence "Glossary of artificial intelligence")

  
  
**Artificial intelligence** (**AI**) is the capability of [computational systems](Computer "Computer") to perform tasks typically associated with [human intelligence](Human_intelligence "Human intelligence"), such as [learning](Learning "Learning"), [reasoning](Reason "Reason"), [problem-solving](Problem-solving "Problem-solving"), [perception](Perception "Perception"), and [decision-making](Decision-making "Decision-making"). It is a [field of research](Field_of_research "Field of research") in [computer science](Computer_science "Computer science") that develops and studies methods and [software](Software "Software") that enable machines to perceive their environment and use [learning](Machine_learning "Machine learning") and [intelligence](Intelligence "Intelligence") to take actions that maximize their chances of achieving defined goals. 

High-profile [applications of AI](Applications_of_AI "Applications of AI") include advanced [web search engines](Web_search_engine "Web search engine") (e.g., [Google Search](Google_Search "Google Search")); [recommendation systems](Recommendation_systems "Recommendation systems") (used by [YouTube](YouTube "YouTube"), [Amazon](Amazon_\(company\) "Amazon \(company\)"), and [Netflix](Netflix "Netflix")); [virtual assistants](Virtual_assistant "Virtual assistant") (e.g., [Google Assistant](Google_Assistant "Google Assistant"), [Siri](Siri "Siri"), and [Alexa](Amazon_Alexa "Amazon Alexa")); [autonomous vehicles](Autonomous_vehicles "Autonomous vehicles") (e.g., [Waymo](Waymo "Waymo")); [generative](Generative_artificial_intelligence "Generative artificial intelligence") and [creative](Computational_creativity "Computational creativity") tools (e.g., [language models](Language_model "Language model") and [AI art](AI_art "AI art")); and [superhuman](Superintelligence "Superintelligence") play and analysis in [strategy games](Strategy_game "Strategy game") (e.g., [chess](Chess "Chess") and [Go](Go_\(game\) "Go \(game\)")). However, many AI applications are not perceived as such: "A lot of cutting-edge AI has filtered into general applications, often without being called AI because once something becomes useful enough and common enough it's [not labeled AI anymore](AI_effect "AI effect")." 

Various subfields of AI research are centered around particular goals and the use of particular tools. The traditional goals of AI research include learning, [reasoning](Automated_reasoning "Automated reasoning"), [knowledge representation](Knowledge_representation "Knowledge representation"), [planning](Automated_planning_and_scheduling "Automated planning and scheduling"), [natural language processing](Natural_language_processing "Natural language processing"), and [perception](Machine_perception "Machine perception"), as well as support for [robotics](Robotics "Robotics"). To reach these goals, AI researchers have adapted and integrated a wide range of techniques, including [search](State_space_search "State space search") and [mathematical optimization](Mathematical_optimization "Mathematical optimization"), [formal logic](Formal_logic "Formal logic"), [artificial neural networks](Artificial_neural_network "Artificial neural network"), and methods based on [statistics](Statistics "Statistics"), [operations research](Operations_research "Operations research"), and [economics](Economics "Economics"). AI also draws upon [psychology](Psychology "Psychology"), [linguistics](Linguistics "Linguistics"), [philosophy](Philosophy_of_artificial_intelligence "Philosophy of artificial intelligence"), [neuroscience](Neuroscience "Neuroscience"), and other fields. Some companies, such as [OpenAI](OpenAI "OpenAI"), [Google DeepMind](Google_DeepMind "Google DeepMind") and [Meta](Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms"), aim to create [artificial general intelligence](Artificial_general_intelligence "Artificial general intelligence") (AGI) – AI that can complete virtually any cognitive task at least as well as a human. 

Artificial intelligence was founded as an academic discipline in 1956, and the field went through multiple cycles of optimism throughout [its history](History_of_artificial_intelligence "History of artificial intelligence"), followed by periods of disappointment and loss of funding, known as [AI winters](AI_winter "AI winter"). Funding and interest increased substantially after 2012, when [graphics processing units](Graphics_processing_unit "Graphics processing unit") began being used to accelerate neural networks, and [deep learning](Deep_learning "Deep learning") outperformed previous AI techniques. This growth accelerated further after 2017 with the [transformer architecture](Transformer_architecture "Transformer architecture"). In the 2020s, an ongoing period of rapid [progress](Progress_in_artificial_intelligence "Progress in artificial intelligence") in advanced generative AI became known as the [AI boom](AI_boom "AI boom"). Generative AI's ability to create and modify content has led to several [unintended consequences and harms](Generative_artificial_intelligence#Concerns "Generative artificial intelligence"). [Ethical concerns](Ethics_of_artificial_intelligence "Ethics of artificial intelligence") have been raised about [AI's long-term effects](AI_aftermath_scenarios "AI aftermath scenarios") and [potential existential risks](Existential_risk_from_artificial_intelligence "Existential risk from artificial intelligence"), prompting discussions about [regulatory policies](Regulation_of_artificial_intelligence "Regulation of artificial intelligence") to ensure [the safety](AI_safety "AI safety") and benefits of the technology.
