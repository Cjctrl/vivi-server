# World War II

World War II (WWII or WW2), also known as the Second World War, was a global war that lasted from 1939 to 1945. It involved the vast majority of the world's countries—including all of the great powers—forming two opposing military alliances: the Allies and the Axis. It was the most widespread war in history, and directly involved more than 100 million people from more than 30 countries.

## Key Events
- **1939**: Germany invades Poland, beginning the war in Europe
- **1940-1941**: German advances in Europe and North Africa; Battle of Britain
- **1941**: Germany invades Soviet Union; Japan attacks Pearl Harbor; United States enters the war
- **1942-1943**: Turning points: Battle of Stalingrad, Battle of Midway, Allied advances in North Africa
- **1944**: D-Day invasion of Normandy; Allied advance into Germany
- **1945**: Germany surrenders (May); Japan surrenders (September) following atomic bombings

## Impact
- **Casualties**: Estimated 70-85 million fatalities, making it the deadliest conflict in human history
- **Geopolitical Changes**: 
  - United Nations established
  - Emergence of United States and Soviet Union as superpowers
  - Beginning of the Cold War
  - Decolonization of Asia and Africa
- **Technological Advances**: 
  - Radar, jet engines, rockets, nuclear weapons
  - Early computers (for code-breaking and calculations)
  - Advances in medicine, logistics, and materials science

## Historical Significance
World War II had a profound impact on the course of human history, shaping the geopolitical, technological, and social landscape of the second half of the 20th century. The war accelerated technological development in many fields, including early computing technologies that laid the groundwork for modern computer science and, eventually, artificial intelligence.

The experiences and lessons from World War II continue to influence international relations, military strategy, and historical understanding to this day.
